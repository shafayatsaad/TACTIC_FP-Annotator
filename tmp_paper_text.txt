We introduce TACTIC-FP, a framework for decoding football tactical intent from broadcast video. The method combines a tri-axial
transformer with a structural causal model that links tactical intent, player movement, and goal probability through a learned directed
acyclic graph (DAG). TACTIC-FP provides three core capabilities: (1) decoding tactical intent 5–10 seconds before critical actions, (2)
detecting real-time deviations from an opponent’s pre-scouted tactical fingerprint, and (3) generating counterfactual scenarios under
the model’s causal assumptions. We present TACTIC-Bench, a 200-match collection with variable-length 2–15-second segments across
12 intent classes; 50 matches have completed triple-annotator validation. On the evaluated split, TACTIC-FP reaches 47.2% TIA@5,
outperforming action-anticipation baselines such as FAANTRA (35.8%) and GPT-4V (23.8%). The tactical deviation model achieves a
CD-AUC of 0.834, and ablations show that agent attention contributes the largest single gain (+13.4 percentage points in TIA@5).
Cross-dataset evaluation on J-League tracking data supports generalization, while the 25M-parameter model enables inference on a
consumer GPU (1.9 clips/s) and a Jetson Nano (0.8 clips/s).

1 INTRODUCTION
1.1 The Problem: What Every Coach Asks
Recent football AI systems can recommend corner-kick receivers with 78% top-3 accuracy [1], forecast in-game outcomes
with axial transformers [2], anticipate actions from broadcast video [3], and generate open-play tactical patterns [4].
However, these systems still do not answer the question coaches ask during a match: "What is the opponent currently
trying to do, and how has that changed from what we prepared?"
a. Causality:CausalTraj [ 5] models temporally coherent multi-agent trajectories, while defensive role recon-
struction methods [6] infer off-ball structure. However, neither line of work learns a causal model connecting
tactical intent, off-ball movement, and outcome probability.
b. Intent Before Action:FAANTRA [ 3] predicts which action will happen but does not explicitly model why it
occurs. For example, a counter-attack and a build-up may produce similar next-action distributions despite
reflecting different tactical intentions.
c. Cross-Phase Coherence:Existing models are not trained jointly over build-up, press, defensive block, and
transition phases, limiting hybrid state understanding and intent representation.

1.2 Core Scientific Claim
We test whether a structural causal model learned from player trajectories derived from broadcast video can decode a
team’s tactical intent 5–10 seconds before critical actions more accurately than action-anticipation baselines, without
proprietary tracking systems.
1.3 Contributions
The six main contributions of this work are:
(1) Structural Causal Model for Tactical Intent:We present a differentiable DAG over tactical intent, movement,
and outcome variables using NOTEARS [7] and structural equation modeling for football tactical analysis.
(2) Tri-Axial Transformer Architecture:We introduce a transformer variant with attention alternating across
temporal, agent, and phase axes. This produces a hybrid representation over temporal context, player interactions,
and game phase.
(3) Counterfactual Tactical Reasoning:We support do-calculus queries over phase, formation, pressing, pitch
control, and xG variables directly from trajectories recovered from broadcast video.
(4) Real-Time Tactical Fingerprinting:We compare live intent latents against pre-scouted team fingerprints
and trigger deviation alerts during a match.

2 RELATED WORK
2.1 Computer Vision for Football
Formal computer vision tasks in football are increasingly organized around the SoccerNet ecosystem [8].
Its benchmark tasks include action spotting, monocular depth estimation, multi-view foul recognition, and game-state
reconstruction. Top teams reach 0.6390 GS-HOTA on game-state reconstruction, demonstrating that tracking from
broadcast video is possible, although noisy, under low-resolution or occluded conditions. Group-activity recognition
reaches approximately 0.50 mAP on collective activities such as counter-attacks, but it operates at the clip level and
does not decode tactical intent.
The SoccerNet pipeline uses YOLOv11 for detection, Deep-EIoU for tracking, PnLCalib for calibration, and OSNet for
re-identification [8]. This literature is the basis of our video-to-trajectory module.
Related work also encompasses counterfactual player valuation [9], football momentum modeling [10], causal framing
in sports [11], graph-based causal discovery [12], dynamic graph attention [13], efficient attention [14], expected-goals
foundations [15], pressing metrics [16], tactic generation [4, 17], and multimodal baselines [18]. These areas inform our
design, but none jointly addresses tactical intent, causal inference, and broadcast video.
2.2 Tactical AI and Causal Inference
TacticAI uses geometric deep learning for corner-kick recommendations but is limited to set pieces and bespoke
tracking data [1]. FAANTRA is strong at action anticipation from broadcast video, yet it remains agnostic to the tactical
approaches that can produce the same distribution of actions [ 3]. TacEleven generates open-play attack tactics [4],
while CausalTraj models temporal but not tactical causality [5].
Causal reasoning in sports requires careful framing of interventions and counterfactuals [11, 19]. NOTEARS intro-
duced continuous optimization for causal discovery with the acyclicity constraint ℎ(𝐴)=tr  𝑒𝐴◦𝐴 −𝑑 [7]; DAG-GNN
and sparse nonparametric DAGs extend this family of methods [12, 20]. Recent tactical behavior reviews show that no
mobile broadcast-only system currently combines causal inference, tactical intent, and real-time fingerprinting [21].
2.3 Multi-Agent Intelligence and Decentralized Systems
We model football as a multi-agent system comprising 22 players and the ball, in which collective behavior emerges
through interactions among agents. Cross-agent attention represents these interactions and their relation to latent
tactical intent. The fingerprint comparator can run locally, without a central server, which helps preserve competitive
privacy. The annotation protocol also supports human–AI collaborative analysis.

3 TASK DEFINITION
3.1 Annotation and Data Pipeline
The TACTIC-FP Annotator is a browser-based tool for assigning and editing tactical labels. Partitions are contiguous,
non-overlapping timeline segments (2–15 seconds). The taxonomy defines 12 tactical intents grouped into five families
(BuildUp, Attack, Press, Transition, and SetPiece) and two exclusions (DeadBall and ContestedPlay). Validation gates
enforce duration bounds, coverage ≥ 80%(at least 18 of 22 players), possession-aware blocking, and exclusion-label
isolation. Each segment records an intent class, confidence score (1–5), and coverage estimate.
Trajectories are created independently using YOLOv11 detection and Deep-EIoU tracking, and matched to segments
according to time. The master tensor [𝑇× 23 × 4] is decimated to 10 fps, and segmented to per-segment tensors
[𝑇seg × 23 × 4] by binary padding with a 150-length mask. For UI testing, a synthetic placeholder is present, which is
not utilized for benchmark metrics.
3.2 DAG Feature Computation
For each non-excluded segment, five trajectory-derived features are computed for the SCM:
(1)Formation Compactness (𝐹):Mean pairwise distance among possessing-team players on the final frame;
(2)Pressing Speed (𝑆):Average velocity of the three closest opponents to the ball carrier;
(3)Pitch Control (𝐶):Voronoi-based area share;
(4) Expected Goal ( 𝑋 ):Distance heuristic 𝑋=clip( 1.0 −𝑑× 2, 0.01, 0.5), where 𝑑 is the minimum ball-to-goal
distance in normalized coordinates;
(5) Phase Mixture (𝑃):4-D softmax over phase prototypes, initialized uniformly during slicing and overwritten
by the transformer during training.
These features feed NOTEARS (Section 4.2).
3.3 TACTIC-Bench Benchmark

4.3 Fingerprint Comparator and Attribution
Each team’s fingerprint is a Gaussian mixture model (GMM; 𝐾= 8, full covariance) fitted to intent latents from
previous matches. During live play, a GMM fitted to segments in a five-minute window is compared with the reference
distribution using the Wasserstein-2 distance. An alert is triggered when 𝑊2 >𝜏 , where 𝜏 is tuned for 90% precision on
the validation set. Wasserstein-2 can compare distributions with non-overlapping supports. Player-level attribution
maps are estimated with Shapley values using𝑀=1000permutations.

4.4 Three-Stage Training Protocol

(500+ matches).
Stage 2 (epochs 41–60): Add phase attention with J-League data (34 matches) for cross-phase coherence.
Stage 3 (epochs 61–100): DAG/SEM fine-tuning with the coverage-weighted loss on TACTIC-Bench. During this step,

6 EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN
6.1 Baseline Models
To ensure that each component of TACTIC-FP is indispensable, we perform an experimental comparison against seven
re-implemented baselines on TACTIC-Bench:
FAANTRA-Adapted[3]: FAANTRA video encoder with a linear classifier on TACTIC-Bench, testing whether action
anticipation is sufficient for intent recognition.TacticAI-style GNN[ 1]: GATv2 on trajectories with standard message
passing, testing geometric deep learning without phase attention.Vanilla Transformer: temporal self-attention only,
isolating the contribution of the tri-axial design.HMM Intent[ 10]: continuous-discrete HMM on trajectory features,
testing classical temporal pattern recognition.Random Forest: hand-crafted PPDA, pitch-control, and formation-
compactness features as a non-deep baseline.NOTEARS-Only: joint transformer training with NOTEARS DAG
regularization, testing end-to-end causal discovery.GPT-4V Tactical[ 18]: 10 sampled frames per segment, testing
whether visual pretraining at scale can learn tactical reasoning without trajectory-level inductive biases.

6.2 Ablation Studies
We ablate each component of our architecture in seven configurations (Figure 10): removing phase attention, removing
agent attention, replacing the SCM with an MLP, replacing the fingerprint comparator with entropy-based detection,
removing cross-phase training, using single-phase models, and replacing NOTEARS with DAG-GNN as a competing
causal discovery method. Statistical rigor is maintained with five random seeds, paired bootstrap tests with 10,000
resamples, and 95% confidence intervals for all primary metrics.
6.3 Validation Protocols
DAG Validation:Synthetic validation uses 1,000 football matches with known causal structure. We report SHD,
TPR, and FDR comparisons against PC, GES, and DAG-GNN baselines. Expert validation presents learned DAG edges
to three UEFA Pro coaches and reports Cohen’s kappa.Cross-Dataset Generalization:We train on the TACTIC-
Bench/SoccerNet data and evaluate on J-League tracking data converted to broadcast-equivalent observations, as well
as held-out matches from the Premier League, La Liga, and Bundesliga.
Statistical Rigor:All primary results are reported as mean ± standard deviation across five random seeds. We
use paired bootstrap tests (10,000 resamples) for TIA improvements and McNemar’s test for paired classification
comparisons. All AUC metrics include 95% confidence intervals.
6.3.1 Data Integrity Validation.A post-annotation validation suite checks for the following:
(1) Zero-duration and sub-2s segments are removed;
(2) Duplicate.npzpaths are eliminated through auto-incremented suffixes;
(3) Excluded segments have no intent assigned;
(4) Each shape in the.npzfile is equal to the corresponding shape in its JSONtensor_shapeand padding mask;
(5) No synthetic DAG features are found in the annotation manifest.
Violations prevent the segment from entering the training dataloader.

7 RESULTS
7.1 Main Results
TACTIC-FP achieves 47.2% TIA@5, which is 5.7 times the uniform-chance level for 12 classes (8.33%). It surpasses
FAANTRA by 11.4 percentage points and GPT-4V by 23.4 percentage points (both 𝑝< 0.001; paired bootstrap with
10,000 resamples). Its CD-AUC is 0.834 on the evaluated split.
Fig. 8. (a) TIA across anticipation windows showing graceful degradation with increased uncertainty. (b) TIA@5 and CD-AUC
comparison across all models. TACTIC-FP leads on all metrics.
7.2 Baseline Comparison and Per-Class Analysis
Action anticipation is insufficient for intent recognition: FAANTRA reaches 35.8% TIA@5 compared with our 47.2%.
The 11.4 percentage-point gap shows that predicting the next action is different from understanding tactical intent.
GPT-4V reaches 23.8%, indicating that visual pretraining without trajectory-level inductive bias is not enough to learn
tactical concepts. The tri-axial design also substantially improves over the vanilla transformer (47.2% vs. 25.8%).

8 DISCUSSION
8.1 Ethical Considerations
Data protection:The system uses broadcast footage and no proprietary club tracking data. Use of broadcast footage
remains subject to the relevant copyright, licensing, privacy, and competition rules; public availability alone does not
imply unrestricted reuse. This work follows the EAIT 2026 “AI for Humanity” principles of analyst augmentation, access
through broadcast-only input, transparency through MIT-licensed code, and explicit evaluation of camera-angle and
geographic bias. The fingerprint comparator runs locally without a central server. The system is intended for sporting
analysis, not gambling applications.
8.2 Societal Impact
Outside football, this framework can generalize to multi-agent domains that require movement-based intent inference,
including autonomous vehicle coordination, crowd behavior analysis, and strategic simulation. The tri-axial transformer

provides a blueprint for multi-agent intent recognition in coordinated systems. In football, TACTIC-FP can make
elite-level tactical intelligence available to clubs that cannot afford proprietary tracking systems, supporting the EAIT
2026 "AI for Humanity" goal of reducing technological inequality.
8.3 Limitations and Future Work
Subjectivity of annotation and decoupled pipeline:The public TACTIC-Bench contains 50 fully validated matches
with triple-annotator review. All remaining matches are interim data points that will undergo secondary review, guided
by a Fleiss’ Kappa of 0.75 (based on the validated subset). Ideally, we want an annotation tool that provides intent labels
without requiring a pre-computed trajectory. Currently, trajectories are generated for each segment and paired by
timestamp during training via YOLOv11 + Deep-EIoU, while a synthetic placeholder is used strictly for UI testing and
excluded from benchmark metrics.
Causal assumptions:Faithfulness and causal sufficiency (no unobserved confounders) are assumed by the SCM.
These are strong assumptions in the context of football, where critical factors like coach instructions, player fatigue, or
psychological momentum are typically unmeasured. To address this limitation, our causal claims are carefully bounded
and supported by sensitivity analysis and synthetic validation.
Camera angle bias: Single monocular broadcast views favor the ball and attacking team; defensive intent may be
underrepresented. Future work should explore multi-camera fusion and camera-angle robustness.
Future work: Future versions should support time-varying DAGs for within-match adaptation and broader geo-
graphic validation. Tactical concepts may differ across leagues and coaching cultures, so additional annotation studies
with non-European coaches are needed.
9 CONCLUSION
TACTIC-FP combines computer vision, causal inference, and streaming multi-agent analysis to decode tactical intent
from broadcast video. We propose a tri-axial transformer for learning sequential patterns of dynamic interactions,
combined with a NOTEARS-based SCM and a GMM fingerprint. On the evaluated split, the model achieves 47.2%
TIA@5 (11.4 percentage points above FAANTRA and 23.4 above GPT-4V), a CD-AUC of 0.834, and a throughput of 0.8
clips/s on a Jetson Nano. By requiring broadcast video rather than dedicated tracking hardware, TACTIC-FP lowers the
infrastructure barrier to tactical analysis. The code, models, and TACTIC-Bench are released under the MIT License.
